The big picture

Not “load CSVs, draw charts”. It is a shared, governed foundation with three ways to consume it: dashboards, a Q&A agent, and role-based access.

S3 and Salesforce are complementary, not alternatives. Both land in Bronze with source_system, file_name or object_name, and load_timestamp.

One key joins everything

patient_mrn is the hub. Vitals also match admissions on patient_mrn + facility_id, and claims link to admission_id.

Why it matters. If MRNs are formatted differently across sources, every join silently loses rows. That is why Silver standardizes MRNs first.

Bronze, Silver, Gold

Security: three tools, three questions

Row filter
“Which rows may I see?” Cardiology staff get Cardiology rows.
Column mask
“What value do I see here?” Billing sees a masked patient_mrn.
Secure view
“What door am I given?” patient_360_secure for patients.

The patient_360 decision

Row filters directly on patient_360 caused nested policy errors because policies referenced mapping tables. Those experiments were discarded. The canonical Gold table stayed clean, and the per-user restriction moved to security.patient_360_secure, matched through security.patient_identity_map. Clinicians use user_department_map and department_row_filter.

Access explorer

Verified in the PDF. MRN000001 vs MRN000002 tests showed one patient cannot retrieve another. Audit result field is response.status_code. Customer-managed keys were not available on Free Edition, so they were not claimed.

The AI layer: retrieve, calculate, explain

Four AI Search indexes (patient_360, appointment, claims, case descriptions) sit in schema ai_search. AI Search retrieves rows; it does not aggregate. Admin questions go to Unity Catalog functions instead.
