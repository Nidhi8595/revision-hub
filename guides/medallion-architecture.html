    Overview

    Source §Overview

    Data comes from many places. Salesforce, databases, APIs, files, applications. It does not always arrive in a usable form.

    • Some records are incomplete.
    • Some are duplicated.
    • Some use different formats.
    • Some need checks or changes before other teams can use them.

    Where do I do all this work?

    I could clean everything the moment it arrives. That has costs. I can lose useful source information. It gets harder to trace where a value came from. It gets hard to process the data again when something breaks.

    So I give the data stages.

    Keep the incoming data first. Improve it step by step. Then prepare the data people actually need.

    This is Medallion Architecture. The common names are Bronze, Silver and Gold. I look at what each boundary does, why it helps, and where it becomes unnecessary.

    Foundations

    Source §2.1–2.7

    What Medallion is

    Medallion is a way of organizing data as it moves through a platform. I give data stages. At each stage I decide what happens to it and what the next users can expect.

    Bronze → Silver → Gold

    The names are not the point. Data becomes more useful as I refine it.

    Medallion is a design pattern, not a specific technology. I can build it with Delta Lake tables, Spark, SQL, pipelines or other tools.

    External The Databricks medallion page also calls it a data design pattern. It says following it is a recommended best practice, not a requirement.

    Why boundaries matter

    A boundary gives a stage a clear responsibility. If I keep source data apart from cleaned data, I know where to look when something breaks. I also decide which transformation belongs in which stage, instead of one large pipeline.

    Where did the data come from? → What was changed? → What can we use it for?

    This matters most when several teams or applications depend on the same data.

    Refinement is not just cleaning

    It is tempting to see three levels of cleanliness. That is too simple. The real idea is progressive refinement. At each stage I make the data more suitable for a purpose. I also try to keep the information and context that later stages may need.

    What Medallion is not

    I keep these separate. They answer different questions. Source §2.4–2.7

    • Not ETL or ELT. ETL/ELT describe how I move and transform data. Medallion describes how I organize the result. Example: ELT into Bronze, clean in Silver, build business datasets in Gold.
    • Not dimensional modeling. Dimensional modeling gives me facts and dimensions. A Gold layer can hold a dimensional model. Gold does not have to mean one.
    • Not Data Vault. Data Vault uses hubs, links and satellites. Medallion does not prescribe a modeling method. I could use Data Vault inside one layer.
    • Not Data Mesh. Data Mesh is about ownership across teams and domains. Domain teams can own data products and still use Bronze, Silver and Gold where they help.

    Architecture & layers

    Source §3.1–3.4 Each layer has a different job. The boundaries stop me from mixing those jobs.

    Bronze: keep what the source gave me §3.1

    Bronze is the first place I land the data. The main idea is source fidelity. I keep a reliable representation of what came from the source. I do not reshape everything at once.

    Example from the source: a Salesforce record arrives with several fields. If I remove fields, change values or combine records before keeping the original, I may not be able to rebuild what actually arrived.

    Bronze gives me a place to go back to. That makes replayability important. If a downstream transformation has a bug, I can potentially run the processing again from what Bronze kept.

    I may also add ingestion details. When the record arrived. Which source it came from. These help me understand the journey of the data.

    The goal is not a perfect Bronze.

    Question
    What did the source provide?
    I can expect Added
    A faithful copy of what arrived, with enough history and metadata to start again.
    I cannot expect
    Clean, deduplicated or standardized data.

    External The Databricks medallion page says Bronze should limit cleanup and validation. It recommends storing most fields as string, VARIANT or binary. This protects against unexpected schema changes.

    Silver: make the data reusable §3.2

    Once the incoming data is preserved, I do the work that makes it easier and safer for other parts of the platform. Validation, deduplication, standardization and schema handling happen here.

    Problems I may find:

    • the same customer appears more than once
    • dates arrive in different formats
    • values use inconsistent naming
    • some records fail basic quality checks

    I fix these once. Downstream users then work with more consistent data.

    Silver is also where I create reusable entities. Instead of every team cleaning customers its own way, I provide one commonly prepared customer dataset.

    Question
    What can downstream teams trust and reuse?
    I can expect Added
    Data that passed the rules I built, in a consistent shape that several consumers can use.
    I cannot expect
    Automatic quality. Silver does not mean perfect. Quality depends on the rules, checks, transformations and monitoring I build.

    External The same page lists Silver work such as schema enforcement, null handling, deduplication, late-arriving data, type casting and joins. It also says Databricks does not recommend writing to silver directly from ingestion.

    Gold: prepare data for a business purpose §3.3

    Gold is more specific to how people or applications use the data. Reporting, analytics, dashboards, business metrics, particular applications.

    Example from the source: instead of giving a business user many customer and transaction records, I give one dataset that supports a sales report.

    Gold does not simply mean "aggregated data". A Gold dataset can be an aggregate. It can also be a specialized dataset, an analytical model, a data mart, or another structure for one use case.

    Question
    What business question does this dataset answer?
    I can expect Added
    A dataset shaped for a named purpose.
    I cannot expect
    Protection or approval. Calling something Gold does not control who can read it.

    Lineage and reprocessing

    Source §3.1, §7.1 Added diagram

    The source says Bronze gives me a place to go back to. If a downstream transformation has a bug, I can potentially process again from Bronze. It warns that a destructive Bronze removes this option.

    External Unity Catalog can track lineage automatically. That is a tool feature. It is separate from the Medallion boundaries.

    Architectural trade-offs

    Source §4

    Why stop at three layers? Why not create more?

    I could add layers for validation, standardized data, shared data, reporting data and so on. But every new boundary has a cost.

    • Another place to store data.
    • More processing to run.
    • More transformations to test.
    • More failures to monitor.
    • More pipelines to manage, and more owners to decide.
    • Latency. Data passes through more steps before it reaches the person or application.
    • Duplicated logic. Without clear responsibilities, the same transformation starts to appear in several places.

    More layers do not automatically mean better architecture.

    The important question is not "Do we have all three layers?" It is "Does each boundary solve a real problem?"

    Patterns & relationships

    Source §5 Medallion does not work alone. I may also use Delta Lake, Unity Catalog, Lakeflow, batch and streaming. They solve different problems. I should not treat Medallion as a technology.

    PartWhat it helps me do
    MedallionOrganize data into refinement stages
    Delta LakeStore and work with tables
    Unity CatalogGovern and manage data
    LakeflowBuild and operate data pipelines
    Data quality practicesCheck whether data meets my expectations

    Delta Lake

    Delta Lake is how I store and work with tables. Medallion is how I organize stages. Bronze, Silver and Gold can be Delta tables. Using Delta Lake does not mean I use Medallion.

    External Delta Lake provides ACID transactions, schema enforcement and time travel.

    Unity Catalog

    Unity Catalog is about governance and metadata. Access, objects, ownership. Having Bronze, Silver and Gold tables does not give me access control.

    Unity Catalog → who can access and manage the data?
    Medallion → how is the data organized as it becomes more refined?

    External Unity Catalog covers access control, discovery, lineage, auditing and more. See What is Unity Catalog?

    Lakeflow

    Lakeflow helps me build and operate pipelines. I can use it to move data through the stages. Medallion does not require it. The pattern describes the organization. The pipeline tool is one way to run the flow.

    External Lakeflow Spark Declarative Pipelines is a declarative framework for batch and streaming pipelines. For files in cloud storage, Auto Loader incrementally processes new files as they arrive.

    ETL / ELT

    ETL and ELT are the work of moving and transforming. Medallion says where that work happens. An ELT process could load into Bronze, refine in Silver, and prepare outputs in Gold. They work together.

    Streaming

    Medallion is not limited to large batches. Data can arrive continuously.

    Incoming events → Bronze → Silver → Gold

    The question stays the same: what should happen to the data at each stage?

    Medallion organizes the data journey. Other technologies and approaches help me store, govern, process and consume that data.

    Databricks perspective

    Source §6

    Medallion fits a lakehouse. It gives me a simple way to organize data from ingestion toward different uses. But I keep the responsibilities separate.

    Medallion is the organization pattern, not the whole Databricks platform.

    • 6.1 Delta Lake. The table and storage foundation. I do not need Bronze, Silver and Gold for every use case.
    • 6.2 Unity Catalog. Governance. The layer tells me the role of the data. Governance decides who can work with it and how. A table called Silver has no automatic access rules.
    • 6.3 Lakeflow. Builds and runs pipelines that move data through the stages. Medallion does not depend on it.
    • 6.4 Batch and incremental. Batch processes a group of records periodically. Incremental or streaming processes data as it arrives. The logical idea stays Source → Bronze → Silver → Gold, whether data arrives once a day or continuously.
    • 6.5 Data quality. Layers give me places for checks and transformations. The name Silver does not guarantee good data.
    • 6.6 Together. None of these makes Bronze, Silver and Gold mandatory. I design around the actual data, users, processing needs and operating cost.

    External The Databricks page also gives an example where customer and transaction data is cleaned by dropping nulls and quarantining invalid records in Silver. I mention it only because it shows quarantine in a medallion flow.

    Anti-patterns

    Source §7.1–7.7 The trouble starts when I treat Bronze, Silver and Gold as rules to follow, not as a way to solve real engineering problems.

    7.1 Destructive Bronze

    I remove fields, overwrite values or make heavy changes before keeping the source. If a transformation was wrong, I cannot process again.

    Instead: do not destroy useful source information before I know I no longer need it.

    7.2 Silver dumping ground

    Validation, business rules, reporting logic, temporary transformations and special cases all pile into Silver. It loses its purpose.

    Instead: keep Silver for data refined into something downstream users can reasonably reuse.

    7.3 Gold duplication

    Several Gold datasets each implement the same business logic. Five dashboards may define "active customer" five ways. Being in Gold does not fix that.

    Instead: find the shared logic and decide where it lives.

    7.4 Unnecessary layers

    I add a layer because the diagram looks more complete. I get another place to store, process, test, monitor and maintain, with no clear gain.

    Instead: a boundary should solve a problem.

    7.5 Duplicated business logic

    The same calculation sits in several pipelines. They can disagree. A rule change needs several edits.

    Instead: keep responsibilities clear so logic has a sensible home.

    7.6 Medallion as governance

    Bronze, Silver and Gold are data organization boundaries. They are not access-control rules. Gold does not mean protected or approved.

    Instead: governance needs its own controls and processes.

    7.7 Silver as automatic quality

    A Silver table is not high quality because of its name.

    Instead: build validation rules, quality checks, monitoring and a way to handle problems. The layer is a place to do the work. It does not do the work.

    A simple decision framework

    Source §7, Decision Framework

    Before I create a new layer, or adopt the full Bronze, Silver, Gold structure, I ask:

    1. Do I need to replay or reprocess the source data?
    2. Do multiple consumers need the same reusable data?
    3. Are the quality and standardization rules big enough to deserve their own boundary?
    4. Do I need business-specific datasets or outputs?
    5. Do different teams or domains need clear ownership boundaries?
    6. Is another modeling approach already solving the problem?
    7. Is the new physical layer worth the storage, compute, testing, monitoring and maintenance it adds?

    These questions help me design around the problem, not around the names Bronze, Silver and Gold.

    Engineering takeaways

    Source, Engineering Takeaways

    Medallion is not three tables with three names. It gives each stage of the data journey a clear purpose.

    • Bronze retains what the source gave me and gives me a place to start again.
    • Silver creates reusable data through validation, deduplication and standardization.
    • Gold shapes data around business needs: metrics, analytics, reporting, specialized datasets.
    • Three layers are not mandatory. A simpler flow may be better.
    • Every physical boundary has a cost: storage, compute, testing, monitoring, maintenance, latency.
    • Medallion differs from the tools around it. Delta Lake, Unity Catalog, Lakeflow and ETL/ELT solve different problems.
    • A layer name does not guarantee quality or governance. Those need real rules, controls and processes.

    I add a data boundary when it gives a clear engineering benefit. Not because the diagram has room for another box.

    Glossary

    Added Short definitions in my words.

    Source fidelity
    Keeping a reliable copy of what the source sent.
    Replayability
    Running processing again from retained data.
    Progressive refinement
    Making data more fit for a purpose at each stage while keeping context.
    Validation
    Checking records against rules, such as required values.
    Quarantine
    Setting invalid records aside with a reason. I do not drop them silently.
    Deduplication
    Keeping one version of repeated records.
    Standardization
    Making formats, names and types consistent.
    Conform
    Aligning data to a shared entity or definition.
    Reusable entity
    A commonly prepared dataset, like customers, that many consumers use.
    Gold dataset
    A dataset built for a business purpose. It may be an aggregate, a specialized dataset, a model or a data mart.
    Physical boundary
    A real separate place for data, with its own storage, processing, tests and monitoring.

    Revision prompts

    Added I try to answer first. Then I open the answer.

    What question does each layer answer?

    Bronze: what did the source provide? Silver: what can we trust and reuse? Gold: what business question are we answering?

    Why is a destructive Bronze painful?

    If a later transformation was wrong, I no longer have enough information to process the data again.

    Name three things Silver does that are not aggregation.

    Validation, deduplication and standardization. Also schema handling and creating reusable entities.

    Why is Gold not simply "aggregated data"?

    It can also be a specialized dataset, analytical model, data mart or another structure built for one use case.

    What does a new physical layer cost me?

    Storage, compute, testing, monitoring, maintenance, ownership decisions and latency. It can also duplicate logic.

    What is the difference between Delta Lake and Medallion?

    Delta Lake is how I store and work with tables. Medallion is how I organize data into refinement stages.

    Does a table named Gold have access control?

    No. Layers are data organization boundaries. Governance needs its own controls, such as Unity Catalog.

    When might Source → Curated be enough?

    For a small, simple use case with few transformation needs. Three physical layers may add more work than value.
